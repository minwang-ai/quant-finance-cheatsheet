# Risk

## Risk Types

| **Risk Type**          | **Definition / Meaning**                                                                                      | **Example / Impact**                                                                                      | **Common Instruments Affected**           |
|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Economic Risk**      | Risk of negative changes in economic conditions affecting businesses or entire economies.                     | Recession, inflation, or currency crisis in a country where you’ve invested.                              | Stocks, Bonds, Mutual Funds, FX           |
| **Equity Risk**        | Risk that stock prices move unfavorably.                                                                      | You buy a stock expecting it to rise, but it falls instead → financial loss.                              | Stocks, Equity ETFs, Mutual Funds         |
| **Interest Rate Risk** | Risk that interest rates change unfavorably, reducing investment/lending value.                               | You hold a 4.5% bond, but new bonds pay 6.4%, so your bond loses value.                                   | Bonds (especially long-term), CDs         |
| **Model Risk**         | Risk that a financial model is wrong or misused, leading to poor decisions.                                   | Using a bad risk model in a trading strategy → unexpected losses.                                          | Derivatives, Structured Products, Quants  |
| **Credit Risk**        | Risk that a borrower fails to repay principal or interest as promised under a **legal/contractual obligation**..                                         | A company defaults on its bond payments; borrower doesn’t repay a loan; bank run                                   | Bonds, Loans, Credit Derivatives (CDS)    |
-------









## Default
### Default (time based stages)
| **Stage**                | **Term**             | **Meaning / Role in Process**                                                                 |
|--------------------------|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 🕐 Payment Due            | —                    | Borrower is expected to pay interest or principal on the scheduled date.                      |
| ⏳ Missed Payment         | Overdue Payment      | A payment not made on time. Could be due to delay, oversight, or temporary financial trouble. |
| ⏱️ Short-Term Window      | Grace Period         | A set number of days after the due date during which payment can still be made without penalty.|
| ⚠️ Continued Non-Payment | Default              | Failure to make payments beyond the grace period. Considered a serious breach of agreement.|
| 📄 Legal Consequences     | Bankruptcy           | Legal process if the borrower can’t repay. Lender may only get part of their money back.   |
----

### Default: Basic Concepts
| **Term**                | **Meaning / Purpose**                                                                      |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Debt Covenant           | Legal conditions the borrower agrees to follow (e.g. max debt limit, minimum revenue). Breaking one can trigger loan recall or default.      |
| Credit Risk             | The lender's risk that the borrower will default.                                           |
| Creditworthiness        | The borrower’s ability and reputation for repaying debts. Bad Creditworthiness -> Bank worries borrower will default → charges higher interest to offset this risk.             |

## Credict Risk
- Credict risk is always an issue for a lender. 
- Credit risk makes finance less predictable: There is a small probability associated with the timing and/or the size of each payment. 

### Credict Risk: Perspectives from Lenders
| **Aspects\\Lenders**       | **Perspective of Investors & Depositors**                                                                                  | **Perspective of Banks**                                                                                       |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Concern**                | - Depositor: bank may fail to return deposits or pay interest (e.g., in a bank run)<br>- Bond holder: issuer may default and fail to pay coupons or principal | Borrower (individual or company) may not repay loan fully or on time                                           |
| **Why This Happens**       | - Banks invest depositor funds but bad investments or economic downturns causes bank failures.<br>- Issuer of bond faces financial distress or mismanagement                                | Income loss, business failure, medical expenses etc                                        |
| **Protection Mechanism**   | - **Deposit Insurance** (e.g., FDIC in U.S. insures up to \$250,000)<br>- **Bond yield compensation**                      | - **No Insurance** <br> Individual borrowers don’t have deposit insurance.  <br>- **Risk-based interest rates**  Their repayment depends on their **creditworthiness**.   Riskier borrowers are charged higher interest rates to compensate for the greater risk.  <br>- **Collateral** <br> see below    |
----


### Mitigation of Credict Risk - Collateral 
| **Concept**          | **Explanation**                                                                                   |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|                       
| **Deifination**       | A **financial or physical or financial asset** that the borrower pledges to the lender as backup for the loan (e.g., car, house, stocks, bonds)            |
| **Purpose** | To reduce the lender’s loss in case of borrower default. If borrower doesn’t pay, the lender can take the collateral. |
| **Bank's Preference** | Banks don’t actually want the car — they prefer timely loan payments, their goal is to minimize defaults and avoid taking over collateral.|
----

### Risk-Free vs Risky Bonds
| **Feature**                  | **Risk-Free Government Bonds**                                          | **Risky Government or Corporate Bonds**                             |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Example Issuers**         | U.S., Japan, Germany                    | Argentina, Russia, Greece; companies             |
| **Default Risk**            | Near zero <br> (assumed in models because of own currency, stable economy)                                           | Non-zero; depends on creditworthiness                               |
| **Yield**                   | Lower <br>(due to low risk)                                                 | Higher <br>(to compensate for credit risk)                              |
| **Common Tools to Avoid Default** | - Raise taxes<br>- Print money<br>- Issue new bonds to roll over old debt | - Cut costs<br>- Restructure debt<br>- Seek external support         |
| **Do These Tools Always Work?** | Only with market trust and moderate use. <br> Printing too much → inflation; excessive rolling over → debt spiral | Often limited or unavailable for companies or weaker countries       |

## Financial Institutions 

### Financial Institutions Types
| **Institution Type**         | **Key Role / Description**                                                                      | **Examples**                             |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Depository Institutions**  | Accept deposits and make loans                                                                  | Banks, Credit Unions, Mortgage Companies |
| **Contractual Institutions** | Offer long-term financial contracts                                                             | Insurance Companies, Pension Funds       |
| **Investment Institutions**  | Manage and originate investments                                                                 | Mutual Funds, Hedge Funds, Investment Banks |


### Financial Institutions: Risk and Regulation
| **Concept**            | **Meaning / Key Idea**                                                                                  |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Multiservice Firms** | Some large financial firms offer **all three services**: banking, insurance, investing                   |
| **Specialized Firms**  | Some firms focus on just one area, e.g., a mutual fund doesn't offer loans or insurance                  |
| **FinTech**            | Technology-driven firms offering efficient alternatives (e.g., money transfers without banking fees)     |
| **"Too Big to Fail"**  | Large institutions are so systemically important that their failure could harm the entire financial system |
| **Regulation Helps** | Ensures stability, reduces risk of collapse, promotes public trust                                     |
